<Step id="scale-driver" title="What drives the scale">

Google reported roughly a billion daily YouTube watch-hours in February 2017. Watch-hours do not determine request rate without assumptions about segment duration, bitrate and client behavior.

This **illustrative** design chooses 1 million segment requests/s globally and a fixed 256 KiB segment. Two regional CDN caches each hold 100,000 segments. Misses reach a US origin shield, which fetches from a European object store. The region matrix charges 35 ms one-way propagation across the ocean. These are invented aggregate budgets, not YouTube's infrastructure.

Bytes and cache locality drive delivery capacity. A hit saves origin work but still consumes edge egress. Both caches start empty; the celebrity scenario uses stationary Zipf s=1.8 from the start, rather than claiming to model a sudden popularity shift.

[Public scale anchor](https://india.googleblog.com/2017/02/you-know-whats-cool-billion-hours.html). All other rates, byte sizes, service times, prices and capacities are illustrative assumptions in the reference design.

<Step id="test-remedy" title="Failure ladder and what to try">

**Observed Failure ladder.** At 4x, the first rung is the US admission-to-CDN edge saturating; the next two rungs are the US and European client-to-admission edges saturating. These are engine edge resources, not evidence of YouTube network limits.

These are the first three rungs of an independent normal-day sweep at **1x, 4x and 16x**, with 2 s warm-up and 8 s measurement per level, fixed 250 ms steps and no sweep drain. Levels report **unstable — no steady state** because the observation window is shorter than the required 30 s. The engine floors edge service time at one microsecond, limiting an edge to roughly one million exchanges/s even when its configured bandwidth permits more. Treat the ladder as a diagnostic for this model.

**What to try.** Try a smaller fixed segment or a lower offered segment rate and compare timely-success and edge utilisation. Separately enlarge cache capacity to observe origin load; that cannot remove the client-facing byte budget. Test the shield brownout and region outage after each change. Remedies are suggestions to test; none is verified here.

The six Scale scenarios are normal day, peak event (4x), US region outage, hot key/celebrity (4x concentrated demand), dependency brownout (10x service time) and DDoS (50x application traffic). Disturbances run from second 4 through second 8; celebrity concentration applies from the start. The outage retains each client's regional entry, with no automatic evacuation. DDoS adds competing traffic without identifying legitimate users.

When sandbox integration is available, select this reference and its normal-day pack in Scale mode, copy it, change one budget, and compare against the unchanged scenario. Check fidelity reasons before reading a disabled metric; correctness stays **unknown**.
