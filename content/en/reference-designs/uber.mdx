<Step id="scale-driver" title="What drives the scale">

Uber reported about 33 million platform trips/day in Q4 2024, including Mobility and Delivery. Trips are not API requests: location updates, quotes and matching attempts can create far more work.

This **illustrative** design chooses 200,000 match operations/s globally with fixed 2 KiB responses. Each regional coordinator calls a local nearby-driver key-value aggregate and a shared US pricing dependency in parallel, then calls US trip storage. Driver-cell membership and prices are abstract service costs; this is not a geographic matching algorithm.

The European coordinator holds capacity while waiting across regions. Even a fast local lookup cannot remove a mandatory remote pricing or commitment round trip. The hot-city scenario concentrates writes on one key; Scale disables contention-sensitive predictions rather than estimating lock behavior.

[Public scale anchor](https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2025/Uber-Announces-Results-for-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024/default.aspx). All other rates, byte sizes, service times, prices and capacities are illustrative assumptions in the reference design.

<Step id="test-remedy" title="Failure ladder and what to try">

**Observed Failure ladder.** At 1x the global timely-success estimate is already below the ladder’s 99% threshold. At 4x the European coordinator api-1 saturates and its bounded queue overflows. This is a finding about this invented cross-region path, not Uber reliability.

These are the first three rungs of an independent normal-day sweep at **1x, 4x and 16x**, with 2 s warm-up and 8 s measurement per level, fixed 250 ms steps and no sweep drain. Levels report **unstable — no steady state** because the observation window is shorter than the required 30 s. The engine floors edge service time at one microsecond, limiting an edge to roughly one million exchanges/s even when its configured bandwidth permits more. Treat the ladder as a diagnostic for this model.

**What to try.** Try placing pricing near the European coordinator, then measure the same scenario and inspect the remaining trip-storage round trip. Increase coordinator capacity only as a separate experiment. Compare the pricing brownout and region outage; use Exact or a focused matching model to investigate assignment correctness. Remedies are suggestions to test; none is verified here.

The six Scale scenarios are normal day, peak event (4x), US region outage, hot key/celebrity (4x concentrated demand), dependency brownout (10x service time) and DDoS (50x application traffic). Disturbances run from second 4 through second 8; celebrity concentration applies from the start. The outage retains each client's regional entry, with no automatic evacuation. DDoS adds competing traffic without identifying legitimate users.

When sandbox integration is available, select this reference and its normal-day pack in Scale mode, copy it, change one budget, and compare against the unchanged scenario. Check fidelity reasons before reading a disabled metric; correctness stays **unknown**.
