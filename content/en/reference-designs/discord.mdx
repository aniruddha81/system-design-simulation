<Step id="scale-driver" title="What drives the scale">

Discord’s March 2023 storage account describes replacing 177 Cassandra nodes with 72 ScyllaDB nodes. Those historical machine counts do not measure channel API throughput or define a universal capacity ratio.

This **illustrative** sketch chooses 200,000 requests/s, 80% history reads and 20% sends, with fixed 1 KiB responses. Each region has channel-message buckets, a durable queue and delivery workers. Read handlers consult a shared US channel directory and local message storage in parallel; sends consult the directory and acknowledge the queue.

One worker operation approximates a mailbox write; websocket fan-out and voice/media traffic are omitted. Consumers use the global read/write mix for service cost. A busy-channel hotspot cannot establish actual shard affinity or lock contention: affected predictions are disabled for that scenario.

[Public scale anchor](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-stores-trillions-of-messages). All other rates, byte sizes, service times, prices and capacities are illustrative assumptions in the reference design.

<Step id="test-remedy" title="Failure ladder and what to try">

**Observed Failure ladder.** At 4x, delivery-0 and delivery-1 saturate. At 16x, the US admission-to-API edge saturates. Background delivery therefore appears before that client-facing edge in this short sweep.

These are the first three rungs of an independent normal-day sweep at **1x, 4x and 16x**, with 2 s warm-up and 8 s measurement per level, fixed 250 ms steps and no sweep drain. Levels report **unstable — no steady state** because the observation window is shorter than the required 30 s. The engine floors edge service time at one microsecond, limiting an edge to roughly one million exchanges/s even when its configured bandwidth permits more. Treat the ladder as a diagnostic for this model.

**What to try.** Try additional delivery concurrency and compare consumer age before changing history-read capacity. Then remove the shared directory dependency in a copy and repeat its brownout and the US region outage. A larger database alone is not a verified fix for worker holding time or a cross-region directory failure. Remedies are suggestions to test; none is verified here.

The six Scale scenarios are normal day, peak event (4x), US region outage, hot key/celebrity (4x concentrated demand), dependency brownout (10x service time) and DDoS (50x application traffic). Disturbances run from second 4 through second 8; celebrity concentration applies from the start. The outage retains each client's regional entry, with no automatic evacuation. DDoS adds competing traffic without identifying legitimate users.

When sandbox integration is available, select this reference and its normal-day pack in Scale mode, copy it, change one budget, and compare against the unchanged scenario. Check fidelity reasons before reading a disabled metric; correctness stays **unknown**.
