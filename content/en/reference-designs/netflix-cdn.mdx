<Step id="scale-driver" title="What drives the scale">

Netflix describes Open Connect partnerships with more than 1,000 ISPs that localize traffic through embedded appliances. That deployment count does not supply a segment API rate.

This **illustrative** design chooses 500,000 requests/s and fixed 1 MiB segments. Each regional LRU cache holds 500,000 segments; misses pass through a US shield to European object storage. Unlike pre-positioned appliance content, these caches start empty and fill on successful misses. The graph teaches delivery locality rather than recreating Open Connect placement or routing.

A cache hit reduces upstream fetches while retaining the full client-facing segment transfer. A fixed segment size also omits adaptive bitrate and real object-size variation. The celebrity pack starts with stationary concentrated popularity, within the cache approximation.

[Public scale anchor](https://openconnect.netflix.com/en/). All other rates, byte sizes, service times, prices and capacities are illustrative assumptions in the reference design.

<Step id="test-remedy" title="Failure ladder and what to try">

**Observed Failure ladder.** At 4x, the US and European admission-to-CDN edges saturate, followed by the US client-to-admission edge. The ladder identifies edge resources before an origin remedy could be treated as a solution.

These are the first three rungs of an independent normal-day sweep at **1x, 4x and 16x**, with 2 s warm-up and 8 s measurement per level, fixed 250 ms steps and no sweep drain. Levels report **unstable — no steady state** because the observation window is shorter than the required 30 s. The engine floors edge service time at one microsecond, limiting an edge to roughly one million exchanges/s even when its configured bandwidth permits more. Treat the ladder as a diagnostic for this model.

**What to try.** Try reducing the fixed segment byte count and compare the same load ladder. Then test a larger cache independently and observe whether origin load changes. Run the shield brownout and US outage with both variants; cache size alone does not verify survival when entry or shield capacity disappears. Remedies are suggestions to test; none is verified here.

The six Scale scenarios are normal day, peak event (4x), US region outage, hot key/celebrity (4x concentrated demand), dependency brownout (10x service time) and DDoS (50x application traffic). Disturbances run from second 4 through second 8; celebrity concentration applies from the start. The outage retains each client's regional entry, with no automatic evacuation. DDoS adds competing traffic without identifying legitimate users.

When sandbox integration is available, select this reference and its normal-day pack in Scale mode, copy it, change one budget, and compare against the unchanged scenario. Check fidelity reasons before reading a disabled metric; correctness stays **unknown**.
