<Step id="scale-driver" title="What drives the scale">

Twitter reported more than 500 million Tweets/day in February 2014. That historical write count is neither a current X traffic claim nor a count of home-timeline reads.

This **illustrative** sketch chooses 300,000 requests/s, 98% reads and 2% posts, with fixed 8 KiB responses. Regional APIs merge a US materialized timeline with European post storage using an all-branches parallel join. Posts persist first, then enter regional fan-out queues; workers update the US timeline aggregate.

Each queued post causes one materialized-timeline operation. Real follower-degree amplification and ordering are absent. Consumer work uses the engine’s global endpoint mix as a cost approximation. A celebrity write therefore does not simulate millions of follower deliveries; hotspot write contention disables affected predictions.

[Public scale anchor](https://blog.x.com/engineering/en_us/a/2014/introducing-twitter-data-grants). All other rates, byte sizes, service times, prices and capacities are illustrative assumptions in the reference design.

<Step id="test-remedy" title="Failure ladder and what to try">

**Observed Failure ladder.** At 1x, the European fan-out worker worker-1 already saturates. At 4x, US timeline API api-0 saturates and its queue overflows. The first bottleneck is background work; a successful foreground post is not evidence that fan-out has caught up.

These are the first three rungs of an independent normal-day sweep at **1x, 4x and 16x**, with 2 s warm-up and 8 s measurement per level, fixed 250 ms steps and no sweep drain. Levels report **unstable — no steady state** because the observation window is shorter than the required 30 s. The engine floors edge service time at one microsecond, limiting an edge to roughly one million exchanges/s even when its configured bandwidth permits more. Treat the ladder as a diagnostic for this model.

**What to try.** Try more European fan-out worker concurrency, then rerun while comparing consumer age and queue depth. Separately move or partition timeline storage to reduce remote worker holding time. Treat hybrid celebrity fan-out as an architectural extension to model, not a remedy already verified by this sketch. Remedies are suggestions to test; none is verified here.

The six Scale scenarios are normal day, peak event (4x), US region outage, hot key/celebrity (4x concentrated demand), dependency brownout (10x service time) and DDoS (50x application traffic). Disturbances run from second 4 through second 8; celebrity concentration applies from the start. The outage retains each client's regional entry, with no automatic evacuation. DDoS adds competing traffic without identifying legitimate users.

When sandbox integration is available, select this reference and its normal-day pack in Scale mode, copy it, change one budget, and compare against the unchanged scenario. Check fidelity reasons before reading a disabled metric; correctness stays **unknown**.
