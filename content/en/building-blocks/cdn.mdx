<Step id="edge-path" title="Serve close to the reader">

<Sequence title="Edge hit and miss" actors={["Reader", "Edge", "Origin"]} messages={[{"from": "Reader", "to": "Edge", "label": "GET /logo.v3.png"}, {"from": "Edge", "to": "Reader", "label": "Fresh cached response on hit"}, {"from": "Edge", "to": "Origin", "label": "Fetch on miss"}, {"from": "Origin", "to": "Edge", "label": "Response with cache policy"}]} />

An edge cache serves reusable responses near readers. A hit avoids the origin round trip. A miss still needs an upstream fetch, so proximity alone does not guarantee fast responses or origin protection.

<Step id="cache-keys" title="Separate representations, not tracking noise">

<Compare title="Cache key inputs" sides={[{"title": "Include when content varies", "points": ["Path and required query fields", "Language or image format"]}, {"title": "Exclude only when irrelevant", "points": ["Tracking parameters", "Unneeded request headers"]}]} />

A cache key identifies a representation. Omitting a meaningful input mixes responses; including irrelevant inputs fragments hits. Respect Vary and the CDN policy. Private or personalized responses generally bypass shared caching; adding a user identifier alone is not a complete privacy policy.

<Step id="ttl" title="Set an explicit reuse window">

<Timeline title="Example: shared-cache TTL of 60 seconds" events={[{"at": "t0", "label": "Edge stores version A"}, {"at": "t10", "label": "Origin publishes version B"}, {"at": "t59", "label": "Edge may still serve fresh version A"}, {"at": "t60", "label": "Entry becomes stale; validate or refetch"}]} />

TTL controls freshness, not physical deletion. s-maxage targets shared caches; max-age also affects browser caches. Once stale, a response may be revalidated rather than fully downloaded. Stale-serving directives can extend reuse, so TTL alone is not an unconditional end-to-end staleness guarantee.

<Step id="shield" title="Gather misses before the origin">

<Sequence title="Two cold edges, one shield" actors={["Edge A", "Edge B", "Shield", "Origin"]} messages={[{"from": "Edge A", "to": "Shield", "label": "Miss for /logo.v3.png"}, {"from": "Shield", "to": "Origin", "label": "Shield miss: fetch once"}, {"from": "Edge B", "to": "Shield", "label": "Later miss for same object"}, {"from": "Shield", "to": "Edge B", "label": "Shield hit: no origin fetch"}]} />

An origin shield adds a shared cache tier between edges and origin. Repeated edge misses can become shield hits. It adds a hop on a shield miss and another operational dependency. Simultaneous cold misses need request coalescing to avoid duplicate origin fetches.

<Step id="purge" title="Publish safely under an old URL">

<Compare title="Update strategies" sides={[{"title": "Versioned immutable asset", "points": ["New content gets new URL", "Update references to new version"]}, {"title": "Invalidate existing URL", "points": ["Evict matching shared entries", "Allow propagation; browser copies may remain"]}]} />

Versioned URLs keep immutable assets reusable while new references select new content. Purges remove cached entries for an existing URL, with provider-specific scope and delay. CDN invalidation does not erase browser copies. Neither method prevents a post-purge surge of misses without origin capacity or coalescing.

<Step id="origin-offload" title="Count misses through both tiers">

<Bars title="Illustrative 10,000 requests/s; edge hit 90%; shield hit 80% of edge misses" items={[{"label": "Arrive at edges", "value": 10000, "unit": "requests/s"}, {"label": "Reach shield", "value": 1000, "unit": "requests/s"}, {"label": "Reach origin", "value": 200, "unit": "requests/s"}]} />

**Origin rate = incoming rate ? (1 ? edge hit ratio) ? (1 ? shield hit ratio on edge misses).** Here 10,000 ? 0.1 ? 0.2 = 200 requests/s: 98% request offload. Byte offload can differ when misses are larger than hits.

<Step id="byte-offload" title="Do not confuse hits with saved bytes">

<Bars title="Ten requests: nine 1 KB hits and one 1,000 KB miss" items={[{"label": "Bytes served from cache", "value": 9, "unit": "KB"}, {"label": "Bytes fetched from origin", "value": 1000, "unit": "KB"}]} />

Request hit ratio counts requests. Byte hit ratio weights response sizes. Nine tiny hits and one huge miss give 90% request hits but only 9 / 1,009 ? 0.9% byte hits. Track both when origin bandwidth is the bottleneck.
