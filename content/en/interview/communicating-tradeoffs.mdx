<Step id="shape" title="The shape of a good trade-off">

Every design choice gives something up. Interviewers listen for whether you know what. A complete trade-off statement has four parts, the same ones an architecture decision record captures: the **option** you chose, what it **gains**, what it **costs**, and the **condition** under which you would choose differently.

"I'll use asynchronous replication to the second region. Writes stay fast, about 5 ms instead of 70. The cost is that a regional failure can lose the last few seconds of writes. For likes that is acceptable; for payments I would replicate synchronously."

<Choice
  id="good-tradeoff"
  objective="name-tradeoff"
  prompt="Which statement is the best-formed trade-off?"
  options={[
    { id: "full", text: "'A cache in front of profiles cuts database reads by about 95%, at the cost of up to 60 s of staleness after an edit. Profiles tolerate that; account balances would not, so they bypass the cache.'", correct: true, feedback: "Right. Option, quantified gain, quantified cost, and the condition that changes the decision." },
    { id: "best", text: "'Redis is the best cache, so I'll use it.'", feedback: "'Best' with no criterion says nothing. Best for what workload, at what cost?" },
    { id: "scales", text: "'Cassandra scales well, so it's the right choice.'", feedback: "A capability without a requirement or a cost. What do you lose, and why does this system need that scale?" },
    { id: "depends", text: "'It depends.'", feedback: "True of every decision, and useless alone. Say what it depends on, then choose." }
  ]}
/>

</Step>

<Step id="quantify" title="Put a number on it">

"Synchronous replication is slower" invites the question *how much slower?* Answer before it is asked. The back-of-envelope skills from the foundations track turn adjectives into numbers: round trips, request rates, bytes, dollars.

<Step id="requirements" title="Trace every decision to a requirement">

The requirements you wrote down at the start are your justification for everything after. When you add a component, name the requirement that forced it. When the interviewer asks "why not X?", compare X against the same requirements.

<Choice
  id="db-choice"
  objective="tie-to-requirements"
  prompt="Your requirements include 'no double-booking of a seat' and 'about 500 bookings per second at peak'. How do you justify the database choice?"
  options={[
    { id: "req", text: "'A relational database with a unique constraint on (event, seat) makes double-booking impossible at the storage layer, and 500 writes per second fits one well-provisioned primary.'", correct: true, feedback: "Right. Each clause answers a stated requirement with a mechanism and a number." },
    { id: "popular", text: "'PostgreSQL is popular and well supported.'", feedback: "Popularity is not one of the requirements. Say which requirement the choice satisfies and how." },
    { id: "nosql", text: "'NoSQL, because it scales to millions of writes per second.'", feedback: "500 writes per second does not need that, and you have not addressed double-booking, the hardest requirement." },
    { id: "both", text: "'Both SQL and NoSQL, to get the best of both.'", feedback: "Two databases double the operational cost and add a consistency problem between them. It needs a reason, not a hedge." }
  ]}
/>

</Step>

<Step id="pushback" title="When the interviewer pushes back">

Pushback is often a probe, not a correction: the interviewer wants to see whether you can reason under challenge. Restate the concern, re-check it against the requirements and numbers, and then either defend your choice with reasons or change it, saying what made you change.

<Choice
  id="pushback-response"
  objective="tie-to-requirements"
  prompt="You chose fan-out on write for timelines. The interviewer asks: 'What happens when a user with 50 million followers posts?' What is the best response?"
  options={[
    { id: "adapt", text: "'That is 50 million inserts per post, so delivery would lag for minutes. I'd treat high-follower accounts separately: skip fan-out for them and merge their posts at read time.'", correct: true, feedback: "Right. You quantified the concern, accepted what it breaks, and adapted the design with a reason." },
    { id: "defend", text: "'It will be fine; the queue will absorb it.'", feedback: "A queue absorbs bursts but does not make 50 million inserts fast. Dismissing a valid concern without numbers is a red flag." },
    { id: "abandon", text: "'You're right, I'll switch everything to fan-out on read.'", feedback: "Folding completely throws away the benefits for the other 99.9% of users. Change only what the concern actually breaks." },
    { id: "deflect", text: "'That's an edge case; let's ignore it.'", feedback: "Celebrities generate a large share of reads and writes. Edge cases at scale are the interview's deep dives." }
  ]}
/>

</Step>
