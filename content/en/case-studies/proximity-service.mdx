<Step id="requirements" title="Requirements">

Find businesses within a given radius of a point (optionally filtered by category), view business details, and let business owners add or edit listings.

**Non-functional**: search p99 under **~100 ms**; heavily skewed toward reads (**~5,000 searches/s** vs **~1 write/s**); high read availability; and **eventual consistency** for search is acceptable (an owner's edit can take a few minutes to appear in radius searches, though the detail page should reflect it right away).

<Step id="estimates" title="Estimates">

Assume **200M businesses** worldwide and **100M daily active users** running 5 searches/day:

- **Search rate**: 500M ÷ 86,400 ≈ **5,800 searches/s** average, **~15,000/s** at peak.
- **Write rate**: ~100k business edits/day ≈ **1 write/s**.
- **Dataset size**: 200M businesses × ~1 KB of details ≈ **200 GB** in the primary database.
- **Geo index size**: 200M × ~30 B (`businessId` + geohash + `lat`/`lng`) ≈ **6 GB** total. That fits in RAM on a single machine!
- **Index replicas**: at ~5,000 queries/s per in-memory replica, **3–4 full replicas per region** carry peak traffic without sharding the index at all.

<Step id="api" title="API">

- `GET /v1/search?lat&lng&radius&category&cursor` (radius ≤ 20 km) → **200** `{ businesses[], next }`.
- `GET /v1/businesses/{id}` → **200** `{ details }`.
- `PUT /v1/businesses/{id}` → **200**. Writes the database and invalidates `biz:{id}` in the cache immediately so the owner sees their edit on the detail page; the geo index catches up asynchronously.

<Step id="data-model" title="Data model">

- **businesses** (`db`): `businessId`, `name`, `geohash` (length 6 ≈ 1.2 km × 0.6 km), `lat`, `lng`, `category`, `rating`, `details`, sharded by `businessId`.
- **geoindex**: an in-memory read-optimised replica mapping `geohash → list of (businessId, lat, lng)`, fed from `db` via change-data-capture (CDC).
- **cellcache**: caches both popular cell search results (`cell:{geohash}`, 60 s TTL) and individual business details (`biz:{id}`).

<Step id="high-level" title="High-level design">

Requests enter through a load balancer to stateless **Search API** servers.
- **Nearby search** uses cache-aside on `cellcache` (`cell:{geohash}`); on a miss it queries `geoindex` for the target cell and its 8 neighbours, filters candidates by exact Haversine distance, caches the result for 60 seconds, and returns.
- **Detail view** uses cache-aside on `cellcache` (`biz:{id}`), falling back to `db`.
- **Owner update** writes `db` and deletes `biz:{id}` from `cellcache`; `geoindex` pulls the change asynchronously.

<Step id="deep-dive" title="Deep dive: boundary effects and adaptive cells">

Two points 10 metres apart on opposite sides of a geohash division line can have completely different prefixes (just as `0999` and `1000` share no prefix in base 10). Querying only the user's own cell misses restaurants right across the street. That is why every search **always queries the centre cell plus its 8 neighbours**, then filters by exact Euclidean/Haversine distance.

Fixed geohash cells have uneven occupancy: one cell in midtown Manhattan holds thousands of cafes while a cell in the desert holds zero. A **quadtree** (or Google's **S2** hierarchy) recursively subdivides a square until every leaf holds at most $k$ businesses — giving tiny leaves downtown and huge leaves in rural areas. <Step id="alternatives" title="Alternatives">

- **No geo index: scan the business table.** The runnable `proximity-scan` alternative removes `geoindex` and queries `db` directly on cache misses. Every miss scans 20,000 rows, saturating the database worker pool and dropping goodput below 90%.
- **Quadtree instead of fixed geohash cells.** Balances candidate count per leaf across dense cities and sparse countryside; built in memory at startup in a few seconds and patched in place. Slightly more code than a B-tree index on a `geohash` column.
- **Database-native spatial index (PostGIS R-tree / GiST).** Zero extra infrastructure and supports arbitrary polygons. Great up to a few thousand queries/s per read replica — often the best V1 before extracting a dedicated in-memory geo index.

<Step id="failures" title="Failure scenarios you can run">

- **Everyone searches Times Square** (30% of searches hit one hot cell on New Year's Eve). The hot cell sits in `cellcache` and costs just one `geoindex` query per minute; the rest of the uniform long-tail misses are easily handled by the index replicas.
- **The geo index is unavailable** (a bad index build kills `geoindex` for 0.5 s). Popular cached cells keep answering from `cellcache` and business detail pages keep working from `db`, while uncached searches time out. Roll out new index builds blue/green beside the old index and swap atomically.
- **Index replicas slow down 5×** (background index rebuild competes for CPU). Cache hits do not notice; misses take ~10 ms longer instead of ~2 ms, and because `cellcache` absorbs the hot cells, `geoindex` stays well below saturation.

<Step id="wrap-up" title="Wrap up">

Recap why this design is so clean: **read-to-write asymmetry (5,000:1)** and a **small spatial footprint (~6 GB)** let every replica hold the full geo index in memory, **9-cell geohash lookups** (or a **quadtree**) solve boundary and density problems, and **cell-level caching** makes viral locations free. Track **search p99 latency**, **cell cache hit rate**, and **CDC index lag**. Score each stage from 1 to 4.
