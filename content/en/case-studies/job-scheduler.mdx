<Step id="requirements" title="Pin down what 'reliable scheduling' means">

A distributed job scheduler runs one-off delayed tasks (`send reminder in 24h`) and recurring cron jobs (`0 * * * *`) across a fleet of workers. The hardest question to clarify right away is **what delivery guarantee is possible when a worker crashes mid-execution**.

**Functional**: register, cancel, and inspect one-off or recurring cron jobs with a payload, retry policy (`maxAttempts`, exponential backoff), and execution timeout; dispatch due jobs to workers; record execution history and route exhausted failures to a dead-letter queue (DLQ).

**Non-functional**: dispatch due jobs within **~1 second** of `scheduledAt` during normal load; guarantee **at-least-once dispatch** (zero lost jobs once committed) combined with **fencing tokens and idempotency keys** so retries look like effectively-once execution; and survive the **00:00 UTC cron spike** without starving latency-sensitive jobs.

<Step id="estimates" title="Size the worker pool and the 00:00 UTC herd">

Suppose tenants register **50 million active jobs** that trigger **500 million executions per day**, with an average job duration of `W = 500 ms`:

- **Average execution rate (`λ`)**: 500M ÷ 86,400 s ≈ **5,800 executions/s**.
- **Average concurrent workers (Little's law)**: `L = λ × W = 5,800/s × 0.5 s =` **2,900 busy worker slots**.
- **The 00:00 UTC spike**: humans love scheduling cron jobs at midnight (`0 0 * * *`) or the top of the hour (`0 * * * *`). If 20% of daily cron runs fire at `00:00:00`, **~100,000 jobs become due in the same second** (~17× average!).
- **Metadata storage**: 50M jobs × 500 B ≈ **25 GB**; 7 days of execution history (`3.5B × 200 B`) ≈ **700 GB**.

<Step id="api" title="Design the submission and lease heartbeat APIs">

- `POST /v1/jobs` with `Idempotency-Key` and `{ schedule, payload, timeoutSec, maxAttempts }` → **201** `{ jobId, nextRunAt }`.
- `GET /v1/jobs/{jobId}/executions` → **200** `{ executions: [{ executionId, scheduledAt, status, leaseEpoch }] }`.
- `POST /v1/executions/{executionId}/heartbeat` with `{ workerId, leaseEpoch }` → **200** `{ leaseUntil }` or **409 Conflict** if the job was cancelled or the lease expired and was reassigned (`leaseEpoch` advanced).
- `POST /v1/executions/{executionId}/complete` with `{ workerId, leaseEpoch, output }` → **200** only if `executions.leaseEpoch == request.leaseEpoch`; otherwise **409 Stale Lease**.

<Step id="data-model" title="Model sharded time buckets and execution state">

Why not let 500 workers run `SELECT * FROM jobs WHERE status='SCHEDULED' AND next_run_at <= NOW() LIMIT 10` on one table? At scale, hundreds of concurrent range scans contend on the same B-tree head.

Instead, partition `jobs` across **$B$ virtual buckets** (e.g., `bucketId = hash(jobId) % 64`):
- **`jobs`**: primary key `(bucketId, jobId)`, composite index **`(bucketId, status, nextRunAt)`**.
- **`executions`**: primary key `executionId = "{jobId}:{scheduledAtEpoch}"`, columns `status` (`ENQUEUED | RUNNING | SUCCEEDED | FAILED | DEAD_LETTER`), `attempt`, `leaseEpoch`, `leaseUntil`.

Because `executionId` is deterministically derived from `(jobId, scheduledAtEpoch)`, even if two scheduler nodes race to enqueue the `12:00` cron tick for `job:99`, the unique primary key on `executionId` guarantees at most one execution row is created!

<Step id="high-level" title="Connect the durable schedule store to the ready queue">

A scalable scheduler separates **waiting for a future timestamp** from **dispatching ready work**:
1. **Submission (`api1`, `api2`)**: deduplicates via `dedup`, writes the job to `jobdb`, and if the job is due immediately (or within the current lookahead window), publishes it to the **Ready Queue** (`readyqueue`).
2. **Bucket Pollers**: each scheduler instance holds a lease on a subset of `bucketId`s (`0..63`), scans `WHERE bucket_id = ? AND status = 'SCHEDULED' AND next_run_at <= NOW()`, atomically creates the `executions` row (and advances `nextRunAt` for cron jobs), and pushes `executionId` to `readyqueue`.
3. **Worker Fleet (`runners`)**: pulls ready messages from `readyqueue`, claims/verifies the execution lease in `execdedup` / `jobdb`, executes the task, and records completion.

<Step id="deep-dive" title="Deep dive: fencing tokens and the 00:00 UTC herd">

### Why visibility timeouts alone are not enough (Kleppmann's fencing tokens)
Suppose Worker 1 claims an execution with a 60-second lease (`leaseEpoch = 1`), starts generating a PDF invoice, and hits a 75-second stop-the-world GC pause (or network stall).
1. At `t = 60 s`, the scheduler sees the expired lease, increments `leaseEpoch = 2`, and dispatches the execution to Worker 2.
2. At `t = 75 s`, Worker 1 wakes up from GC and tries to write its output to storage and mark the execution `SUCCEEDED`.
3. Because both the storage service and `jobdb` check `WHERE lease_epoch = :token`, Worker 1's write carrying `leaseEpoch = 1` is rejected (`1 < 2`). The monotonic fencing token stops the zombie worker cold!

### Surviving 00:00 UTC
When 20% of all daily cron jobs specify `0 0 * * *`:
- **Hash-jittered cron (`H 0 * * *`)**: encourage or default recurring schedules to spread deterministically by `hash(jobId) % 3600` seconds unless exact wall-clock second is required.
- **Priority lanes**: keep separate ready queues for `HIGH` (real-time user webhooks, SLA under 1 s) and `BATCH` (nightly reports, SLA under 10 min) so midnight batch jobs never delay user-facing tasks.

<Step id="alternatives" title="Compare the alternatives">

- **No execution idempotency or fencing (`scheduler-no-fencing`)**: runnable in the simulator. When the worker fleet crashes in `runner-crash` and in-flight jobs are redelivered after the visibility timeout, appending results without `execdedup`/fencing causes `duplicateEffect` violations.
- **Unindexed table scan (`scheduler-table-scan`)**: runnable in the simulator. Polling the jobs table without an index saturates `jobdb` and drops goodput below 70%.
- **In-memory hierarchical timing wheel**: O(1) insertion and tick advancement in RAM (used inside Kafka and Netty for request timeouts), paired with a durable database lookahead loader that pre-fetches jobs due in the next 60 seconds.

<Step id="failures" title="Run the failure scenarios">

1. **Worker fleet crash (`runner-crash`)**: `runners` die from t = 1.3 s to 1.8 s. Unacknowledged executions reappear after the 400 ms visibility timeout and are re-executed cleanly once workers recover, with `execdedup` preventing any `duplicateEffect` violation.
2. **Midnight cron spike (`midnight-cron-spike`)**: traffic triples at the top of the hour. `readyqueue` buffers the surge and feeds `runners` at steady capacity with >99% goodput.
3. **Jobs DB failover (`db-leader-failover`)**: `jobdb` leader crashes at t = 1.5 s. Cached execution status reads keep working while a replica is promoted in ~0.5 s with leader fencing enabled.

<Step id="wrap-up" title="Wrap up and self-assess">

Close your interview by naming the two SLIs that matter most for a job scheduler:
- **Dispatch lag (`startedAt - scheduledAt`)**: p99 under 1 s for real-time priority queues; alert if any `bucketId` poller falls behind `NOW()`.
- **Dead-letter rate & zombie rejection rate**: alert tenants when jobs enter the DLQ, and track `409 Stale Lease` rejections to spot jobs whose execution time exceeds their configured lease/heartbeat window.
