<Step id="requirements" title="Requirements">

Users publish posts and read a feed of recent posts from the accounts they follow, newest first, paginated. Feed reads p99 under 200 ms; reads outnumber posts about 100:1; a new post should appear in followers' feeds within a few seconds. **Posting must stay fast and available even when the feed pipeline lags** — staleness is acceptable, errors are not.

<Step id="estimates" title="Estimates">

- **Feed reads**: 300M daily users × 10 loads ÷ 86,400 ≈ **35k/s**, about 70k/s at peak.
- **Posts**: 300M × 0.1 ÷ 86,400 ≈ **350/s**.
- **Fan-out writes**: 350 posts/s × ~200 followers ≈ **70k feed inserts/s** — a 200× write amplification.
- **Feed storage**: 300M feeds × 500 post IDs × 8 B ≈ **1.2 TB** of IDs. Feeds store IDs, not bodies.
- **A celebrity post**: one account with 50M followers is 50M inserts — at 70k/s, about 12 minutes of the entire fan-out tier.

<Step id="api" title="API">

- `POST /v1/posts` with an `Idempotency-Key` (or a client-generated post ID) → 201.
- `GET /v1/feed?cursor=` → 200 with posts and `nextCursor`. The cursor encodes the last post ID seen.
- `POST /v1/follows/{userId}` → 204, optionally backfilling recent posts.

**Cursor pagination** stays stable while new posts arrive. Offsets do not: if five posts arrive between page 1 and page 2, offset 20 now shows five posts the user already saw.

<Step id="data-model" title="Data model">

- **posts**: the source of truth, sharded by author, so "posts by X" is one shard.
- **feeds**: one precomputed list of post IDs per user (newest first, capped at ~500) in a key-value store. Post bodies are hydrated from a post cache at read time.
- **follows**: follower → followee, indexed both ways, read by the fan-out workers.

<Step id="high-level" title="High-level design: fan-out on write">

Posting writes the post and publishes one message to a **fan-out queue**, then returns 201. Fan-out workers read the follower list and write the post ID into each follower's feed. Feed reads go through a cache to the feed store: one key lookup per read.

The queue is what keeps posting fast: a surge in posts becomes a deeper queue and a few seconds of extra staleness, not slow or failed requests.

<Step id="deep-dive" title="Deep dive: push, pull or hybrid">

Fan-out on write costs posts/s × average followers in writes, but a read is one lookup. Fan-out on read costs feed reads/s × accounts followed in lookups, but posting is free. With 100 reads per post and a few hundred followers, push wins for ordinary accounts. A celebrity breaks push, so **hybrid** systems skip fan-out above a follower threshold and merge celebrity posts at read time (see the Twitter timeline case study). Inactive users can be skipped during fan-out and rebuilt on their next visit.

<Step id="alternatives" title="Alternatives you can run">

- **Fan-out on read.** Each feed read becomes a scatter-gather of indexed queries, one per posts shard. In the simulator that is 8 queries per read; the posts database cannot keep up and most reads fail on the same hardware that serves the push design comfortably.
- **Appending post IDs instead of idempotent writes.** Natural, but a worker that crashes after writing and before acknowledging gets the message again and appends the post twice. Run the worker-crash scenario against it: the engine reports a duplicated effect.

<Step id="failures" title="Failure scenarios you can run">

- **A 4× surge.** Posts go from 150/s to 600/s, above the workers' ~400/s. Posting stays fast; the fan-out queue absorbs the excess and consumer lag rises to about a second, then drains.
- **Fan-out workers crash** for 0.6 s. Feeds stop updating, then catch up; in-flight messages are redelivered and the idempotent writes create no duplicates.
- **The feed cache is flushed.** Reads fall through to the feed store, which is sized for them. Precomputed feeds make a cache loss cheap.

<Step id="wrap-up" title="Wrap up">

Summarise: push fan-out through a durable queue, idempotent feed writes, feeds of capped ID lists hydrated from caches, cursor pagination, a celebrity threshold with read-time merge. Set a **freshness SLO** on fan-out lag and alert on it. Then score each stage from 1 to 4.
