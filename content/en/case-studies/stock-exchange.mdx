<Step id="requirements" title="Pin down exchange fairness and determinism">

A stock exchange matching engine is the ultimate test of **deterministic ordering**. Every millisecond, market makers and retail brokers submit limit orders, market orders, and cancellations. Regulators and traders require two ironclad guarantees:
1. **Price-time priority (FIFO)**: the highest bid (buy) and lowest ask (sell) always match first; among orders at the same price, **the order sequenced earlier must fill first**.
2. **Determinism**: given the exact same sequence of input commands, any replica of the matching engine must produce the exact same trades and order book state down to the last share.

**Functional**: accept `NewOrderSingle` (`BUY`/`SELL`, `symbol`, integer `priceTicks`, `qty`, `clOrdId`) and `OrderCancelRequest`; match crossing orders immediately; emit `ExecutionReport` fills to the counterparties and L1/L2 market data updates to subscribers.

**Non-functional**: sub-millisecond to low-millisecond matching latency; **zero out-of-order executions (`orderingViolated`)** per symbol; **idempotent order entry** on `clOrdId`; and instant failover without losing a single sequenced command.

<Step id="estimates" title="Show that the entire order book fits in L3 cache and RAM">

Across **8,000 listed symbols** during a **6.5-hour (23,400 s) trading day**:

- **Order & cancel volume**: 5 billion messages/day ÷ 23,400 s ≈ **210,000 messages/s** average, peaking at **~1,000,000 messages/s** at the 9:30 AM open and 4:00 PM close. (Note: **85–90% of all messages are cancellations and replacements** from market makers!)
- **Active resting orders in the book**: ~10 million open orders across all symbols. At **64 bytes per `OrderNode`** (`orderId`, `priceTicks`, `remainingQty`, `prev*`, `next*`, `participantId`), the entire exchange's resting order book is `10M × 64 B =` **640 MB of RAM** — or under **80 KB per symbol** (fits inside L2/L3 CPU cache!).
- **Sequencer log**: 5B messages × 64 B binary frame ≈ **320 GB/day**, sequential append.

<Step id="api" title="Design the order gateway and market data protocols">

Real exchanges use compact binary protocols (FIX / SBE / Nasdaq OUCH for order entry; ITCH over UDP multicast for market data), which we map to two simulated endpoints:
- `POST /v1/orders` (`NewOrderSingle`): `{ clOrdId, symbol, side: "BUY"|"SELL", priceTicks, qty }` → deduplicated on `clOrdId`, checked against pre-trade risk limits, sequenced, and matched.
- `DELETE /v1/orders/{ordId}` (`OrderCancelRequest`): removes the resting quantity from the book in $O(1)$ time.
- `GET /v1/marketdata/{symbol}/book`: returns top-of-book (L1) and price-level depth (L2), served from the low-latency market data cache fed by the matching engine.

Always represent prices as **integer ticks** (e.g., `priceTicks = 18542` for `\$185.42` at a `\$0.01` tick size), never IEEE 754 floats (`0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004`), so arithmetic is exact and deterministic across every CPU.

<Step id="data-model" title="Build an O(1) in-memory limit order book">

Because 90% of messages are cancels and matches always happen at the best bid/ask, every book operation must be $O(1)$:
1. **Two sides (`bids` sorted descending, `asks` sorted ascending)**: each price maps to a `PriceLevel` struct holding `totalVolume` and the `head`/`tail` pointers of a **doubly-linked list** of `OrderNode`s in FIFO sequence order.
2. **Fast `orderId` lookup**: a pre-allocated hash map (or flat array indexed by sequence slot) maps `orderId → OrderNode*`.
- **Add resting limit order**: append `OrderNode` to the tail of its `PriceLevel` doubly-linked list and insert `orderId → OrderNode*` in the map — **$O(1)$** once the price level is located.
- **Match incoming order**: walk `head` of the best opposite `PriceLevel` list, decrementing `qty` and unlinking filled `head` nodes — **$O(1)$ per resting order filled**.
- **Cancel by `orderId`**: look up `OrderNode*` in the hash map and splice out `node->prev` and `node->next` in the doubly-linked list — **strictly $O(1)$** regardless of whether 50,000 orders sit at that price!

<Step id="high-level" title="Connect gateways, the sequencer log, and the matching engine">

1. **Order Gateways (`gw1`, `gw2`)**: terminate client connections, deduplicate `clOrdId` in `dedup`, and verify pre-trade risk limits.
2. **Deterministic Sequencer Log (`sequencer`)**: partitioned by `symbol` (`orderedByKey: true`). Assigns a gapless monotonic sequence number to every command and replicates it to a consensus quorum before delivery.
3. **Single-Threaded Matching Engines (`matcher`)**: each partition is consumed in strict sequence order by its matching engine, which updates the in-memory book and writes execution reports and book snapshots to `bookstore` and `mdcache`.

### Why single-threaded beats multi-threaded for a symbol
If 8 worker threads concurrently pull orders for `AAPL` from a queue:
- Even a 1-millisecond pause or lock stall on Thread 1 (holding Sequence #101: `BUY 100 AAPL @ $200`) lets Thread 2 (holding Sequence #102: `BUY 100 AAPL @ $200`) reach the order book first and steal the fill!
- That violates price-time priority (`orderingViolated` in the simulator) and means two replicas of the engine will produce **different trades** from the same input log.
- Instead, follow the **LMAX Disruptor** pattern: pin one single-threaded matching loop per symbol shard to a dedicated CPU core with zero locks, zero heap allocations (pre-allocated ring buffer), and zero I/O in the loop. One core executes ~2 million matches/s, and symbols scale horizontally across cores (`AAPL..D` on Core 1, `E..L` on Core 2).

<Step id="alternatives" title="Compare the alternatives">

- **Concurrent workers with variable latency and short visibility timeouts (`exchange-unordered-workers`)**: runnable in the simulator! When `matcher` runs with `concurrency: 8`, bimodal latency (`100 µs` vs `4,000 µs`), and a `1 ms` visibility timeout on `hot-symbol`, fast orders overtake slow/redelivered orders on the same symbol and trigger **`orderingViolated`**.
- **Synchronous SQL matching (`exchange-sql-matching`)**: runnable in the simulator. Matching via indexed SQL queries and writes saturates the database and drops goodput below 70%.
- **Active-active state machine replication vs post-match DB replication**: replicating the 64-byte input command at the sequencer before matching is smaller, faster, and guarantees the hot standby has the exact in-memory book ready for sub-millisecond takeover.

<Step id="failures" title="Run the failure scenarios">

1. **Hot earnings symbol (`hot-symbol`)**: 80% of all order traffic targets a single ticker symbol. The reference design processes the symbol's partition in strict sequence order with zero `orderingViolated` errors and >99% goodput (whereas the concurrent-worker alternative fails with `orderingViolated`).
2. **Market open burst (`market-open-burst`)**: traffic doubles at the 9:30 AM opening bell. Gateways, sequencer, and in-memory matcher absorb 6,000 rps well inside the 200 ms deadline.
3. **Market data cache restart (`md-cache-flush`)**: `mdcache` flushes at t = 1.5 s. Order matching is completely decoupled from market data readers and continues without a blip while the cache refills.

<Step id="wrap-up" title="Wrap up and self-assess">

Close your exchange design with the operational guardrails that real venues enforce:
- **Cancel-on-disconnect & kill switch**: if a market maker's gateway session drops, automatically inject `CancelAll` commands into the sequencer for their open quotes so stale quotes are not picked off while they are offline.
- **Price collars & circuit breakers (LULD)**: reject fat-finger orders more than X% away from the reference price at the gateway, and pause matching on a symbol if its price moves beyond regulatory bands within 5 minutes.
